4. THE MATH (keep it exactly like this, for truthfulness)
----------------------------------------------------------------
For a picked company:

  WORKER
  1. Find the worker's income group: the highest quintile whose lower_limit
     <= the company's median worker pay (quintile 1 if below quintile 2's
     lower limit).
  2. needs = that quintile's necessities (food + housing + transportation
     + healthcare), in dollars per year.
  3. workerShare = needs / median worker pay.
     If workerShare > 1, say "more than their whole pay": low-income
     households often spend more than they earn (in 2021 the lowest group
     spent $30,869 on everything with $13,165 of income).

  CEO (an estimate, and labeled as one)
  4. ceoNeeds = the HIGHEST quintile's necessities (the closest data we have).
  5. ceoShare = ceoNeeds / CEO pay.
  6. Always say: "estimate: the top 20% of households is the closest group in
     the data. CEOs likely spend more, but it would still be a tiny share of
     their pay."

Do not round until the number is shown. Show shares to 1 decimal place
(0.1% for the CEO is fine, use "<0.1%" if smaller).

6. CAVEATS TO SHOW (small gray note under the chart)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  - Spending is the average for U.S. households in the same income group,
    from the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey (2021). Households can have more
    than one earner, so this is an estimate for one worker's pay.
  - The CEO's share uses the top 20% of households (the highest group in the
    data), so it's an estimate. CEOs likely spend more.
  - Basic needs here = housing, food, transportation and health care.
